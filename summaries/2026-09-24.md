# Congressional Summary (run on 2026-09-24)

[https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-150/daily-digest](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-150/daily-digest)

The Senate convened on Tuesday, September 22, 2026, to continue deliberations on the Protect College Sports Act (S. 4668), legislation designed to safeguard student-athlete name, image, and likeness rights while promoting fair intercollegiate competition. The chamber achieved cloture on the Thune amendment by a vote of 70-21, clearing the way for post-cloture consideration to resume Wednesday morning. Beyond the college sports legislation, the Senate passed several bills including the Vicksburg National Military Park Boundary Modification Act and the Reversionary Interest Conveyance Act, approved resolutions recognizing Hispanic Heritage Month and National Purple Heart Recognition Day, and appointed members to the Congressional Award Board.

Numerous amendments to the college sports legislation remain pending, reflecting deep divisions over athlete compensation, conference operations, and private equity involvement. Amendments address concerns including foreign financing disclosure, private equity prohibitions, coaching compensation caps, and support for historically Black colleges and universities. The House remained out of session while the Senate scheduled debate to resume Wednesday at 10 a.m., with a confirmation vote at 2:15 p.m. for judicial nominee Angela Veronica Colmenero.

Proposed amendments address diverse regulatory approaches to college sports governance. Senators Moody, Tuberville, Kennedy, and Armstrong submitted amendments covering conference consolidation restrictions, private equity firm prohibitions, student athlete eligibility standards, and antitrust liability clarifications. Senator Armstrong's amendment notably included unrelated provisions addressing natural gas pipeline permitting and hardrock mining operations, alongside amendments to streamline environmental reviews under the National Environmental Policy Act.

Additional amendments from Senators Scott, Blumenthal, and Blackburn focus on enforcement mechanisms and institutional protections. These amendments establish foreign financing disclosure requirements, prohibit certain foreign investments in intercollegiate athletics, restrict harmful commercial partnerships in name and likeness agreements, and expand private equity prohibitions to include foreign sovereign wealth funds. Senator Blackburn's amendment preserves sovereign immunity rights for public institutions of higher education in the legislation's implementation.

The Senate also received executive communications from federal agencies regarding banking oversight, geothermal and mineral leasing, and oil and gas operations. Additionally, the Senate accepted South Carolina's application for an Article V Constitutional Convention to establish congressional term limits while Washington State rescinded its prior applications.
