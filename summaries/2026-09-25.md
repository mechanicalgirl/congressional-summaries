# Congressional Summary (run on 2026-09-25)

[https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-151/daily-digest](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-151/daily-digest)

The Senate convened on September 23, 2026, to continue consideration of S. 4668, the Protect College Sports Act, while also passing the Satellite Cybersecurity Act and the Secure Space Act. The chamber confirmed one district court judge and approved numerous military and routine nominations before adjourning at 7:28 p.m. as a tribute to former Senator Jon Llewellyn Kyl of Arizona. On September 24, the Senate scheduled a reconvening at 10 a.m. to continue voting on amendments to the college sports legislation, with multiple committee meetings planned to address defense budget matters, HHS and Treasury nominations, ambassador confirmations, and judicial nominations involving topics ranging from women's sports protections to artificial intelligence surveillance.

Senator Kaine submitted twelve resolutions requesting detailed human rights reports from the Secretary of State regarding practices in twelve countries including Belize, Burundi, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Ecuador, El Salvador, Uganda, and Moldova. These resolutions seek comprehensive information within thirty days covering alleged human rights violations such as arbitrary detention, torture, extrajudicial killings, and human trafficking, with particular emphasis on the treatment of non-citizens removed by the U.S. government and the potential misuse of American security assistance. The Senate Armed Services Committee reported favorably on extensive military nominations spanning multiple service branches, with hundreds of officers at various ranks recommended for confirmation, though several were flagged to require nominees to commit to appearing before Senate committees as requested.

Senate debates centered on contested policy measures and approvals of less controversial bills. Senator Lee introduced voter identification requirements with claims that eighty-three percent of Americans support photo ID at polling places, but Senator Padilla objected on grounds that implementation weeks before elections would create administrative chaos. Senator Marshall similarly sought unanimous consent for the Patients Deserve Price Tags Act requiring healthcare cost disclosure, but Senator Paul objected, arguing that price transparency would have minimal impact since insurance companies already negotiate fixed rates. In contrast, the Senate passed several uncontroversial bills including a Romance Scam Prevention Act, a playground redesignation resolution in West Virginia, New Mexico land grant legislation, and a Critical Infrastructure Manufacturing Feasibility Act.
