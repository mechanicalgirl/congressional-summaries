# Congressional Summary (run on 2026-07-25)

[https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-121/daily-digest](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-121/daily-digest)

On July 23, 2026, the Senate advanced consideration of H.R. 5334, the Seed Act, which would extend educator expense deductions to early childhood educators, and confirmed two federal judges to their positions through largely party-line votes: Daniel Mack Traynor to the Eighth Circuit Court of Appeals and Antonio M. Pozos to the Eastern District of Pennsylvania. The chamber also established a timeline for a cloture vote on Walter Clayton's nomination as Director of National Intelligence and advanced several other executive nominations through committee. Meanwhile, the House passed two significant bills on the same day, including H. Con. Res. 89 directing the President to withdraw armed forces from Iran pursuant to the War Powers Resolution in a 214-208 vote, and H.R. 8884, the Removing Barriers to Work for Disabled Americans Act, which would reauthorize Social Security Administration demonstration projects testing program changes to encourage disabled workers to return to employment.

The House debate over H.R. 8884 reflected deep partisan divisions regarding disability benefits and Social Security policy. Republicans argued the legislation addresses a critical gap between disabled Americans willing to work and those successfully returning to employment, citing combat-wounded veterans facing benefit cliffs that discourage workforce participation. Democrats strongly opposed the measure, claiming it grants the Trump administration excessive authority to experiment with disability benefits without adequate protections and potentially worsens customer service issues at the SSA due to recent staffing cuts. They expressed particular concern that the bill's language protecting total income rather than actual disability benefits could enable significant reductions in benefits themselves. The House ultimately passed the legislation on a party-line vote of 232-188.

Beyond these primary legislative matters, the Senate engaged in substantive debates on foreign policy, artificial intelligence regulation, and allegations of presidential corruption. Democrats characterized the ongoing conflict with Iran as illegal and economically damaging through elevated energy prices and depleted military stockpiles, while Republicans emphasized national security threats posed by the Iranian regime. Senator Bernie Sanders delivered a lengthy critique of unregulated artificial intelligence development by billionaires, warning of threats to employment, children's welfare, privacy rights, and democratic institutions. Senator Adam Schiff presented an extensive summary of alleged corruption within the current administration, detailing approximately $6 billion in self-enrichment schemes.
