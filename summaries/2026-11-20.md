# Congressional Summary (run on 2026-11-20)

[https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-175/daily-digest](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-175/daily-digest)

The Senate continued post-cloture consideration of H.R. 2347, the Survivor Justice Tax Prevention Act, while the House passed two measures under suspension of the rules: H.R. 5490, the Dismantle Foreign Scam Syndicates Act, addressing transnational criminal scams targeting Americans, and H.R. 7934, the Settlement Agreement Information Database Act, requiring publication of settlement agreements. Both chambers conducted numerous committee hearings on topics ranging from federal nominations to cybersecurity challenges for small businesses, and both are scheduled to reconvene on November 19.

Recent Congressional activity has centered on healthcare policy and government transparency. Democrats launched an extended discussion on healthcare policy, arguing that Republican legislation will cause approximately 51,000 preventable deaths annually while removing coverage from millions. The House passed the Settlement Agreement Information Database Act on a bipartisan basis, requiring federal agencies to publish detailed settlement information exceeding ten million dollars in a centralized, publicly searchable database to increase government transparency and prevent selective disclosure of information.

The House passed H.R. 5490 with strong bipartisan support after a 48-0 committee vote, establishing an interagency task force to combat transnational criminal organizations perpetrating large-scale online scams against Americans. The legislation addresses rapidly growing threats from Chinese criminal syndicates operating forced labor compounds in Southeast Asia, with Americans losing at least $10 billion to these operations in 2024 alone. The bill requires the Secretary of State to chair a task force developing a comprehensive strategy within 240 days and grants the President authority to impose sanctions on perpetrators while supporting trafficking victims.

Senator Durbin and colleagues introduced the Student Loan Borrower Bill of Rights, which comprehensively restructures protections for federal and private student loan borrowers. The legislation significantly expands disclosure requirements, establishes rigorous servicing standards, creates consumer protections including late fee caps and prohibitions on predispute arbitration agreements, and mandates special protections for private education loans such as discharge provisions for death or disability. The bill also creates an interagency working group to coordinate servicing standards and expands the Education Loan Ombudsman's jurisdiction to cover all postsecondary loans.
