# Congressional Summary (run on 2026-09-04)

[https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-139/daily-digest](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-139/daily-digest)

On Wednesday, September 2, 2026, the House of Representatives voted on several measures, including a failed constitutional amendment to fix Supreme Court justices at nine and a successful passage of the Protecting Domestic Mining Act. The chamber swore in newly elected Representative Aisha Wahab from California's 14th District, bringing total House membership to 433, while the Senate remained in recess and is scheduled to reconvene Friday in a pro forma session.

Numerous House committees held hearings and markups on diverse topics including nuclear energy policy, USPTO fee-setting authority, birthright citizenship, natural resources management, small business opportunities, and veterans' affairs modernization. Members also delivered extensions of remarks paying tribute to individuals and organizations from their districts and providing personal explanations for missed votes.

Democratic members held an extended Special Order hour defending diversity, equity, and inclusion initiatives against the Trump administration's opposition to such programs. Led by Representative Jackson of Illinois and featuring speakers including Representatives Raskin, Tlaib, and Stansbury, Democrats argued that DEI programs address systemic inequities and historical discrimination while providing crucial safeguards for voting rights, healthcare access, and equal economic opportunity. Representative Raskin criticized the administration's characterization of qualified military leaders as "DEI hires," contrasting their credentials with administration officials he termed "DUI hires."

The House debated H.J. Res. 1, a constitutional amendment to permanently fix the Supreme Court at nine justices. Republicans, led by Representative Biggs, argued the amendment is necessary to prevent court-packing and preserve judicial independence, while Democrats, led by Representative Raskin, countered that Congress has historically possessed constitutional authority to adjust the Court's size and that Republicans are seeking to entrench a conservative majority created through questionable confirmation processes, particularly referencing the contrasting treatments of Merrick Garland's and Amy Coney Barrett's nominations.

The debate revealed fundamental disagreements about constitutional interpretation and political power, with Republicans warning that codifying nine justices would prevent future court-packing arms races and Democrats arguing that freezing the number contradicts the Framers' intent and historical practice, noting the number has changed seven times previously.
