# Congressional Summary (run on 2026-09-30)

[https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-153/daily-digest](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-153/daily-digest)

The Senate passed the Protect College Sports Act on Monday, September 28, establishing a federal framework for name, image, and likeness rights, transfer rules, and revenue sharing for student-athletes while limiting NCAA antitrust protections. The legislation passed 77-22 after debate and voting on several amendments, with supporters emphasizing protections for over 500,000 student-athletes and opponents arguing it inadequately enforces athlete rights. The act includes mandatory agent registration, medical coverage guarantees, transfer eligibility protections, and creation of a Student Athlete Ombudsman office, while also modifying broadcasting rules for media rights sales.

Senators Cruz and Cantwell submitted a comprehensive amendment to establish the Weather Research and Forecasting Innovation Reauthorization Act of 2026, substantially expanding the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration's capabilities through fiscal year 2031. The amendment authorizes new initiatives for artificial intelligence-based weather modeling, modernizes radar systems, enhances coastal and flood forecasting, and expands commercial weather data partnerships while maintaining government-operated satellite systems. Key provisions include $25 million annually for NOAA Weather Radio operations and $100 million for modernization, improved hazard communication programs, expanded coverage to underserved areas, and research on how the public interprets and responds to weather warnings.

Two Senate amendments addressed higher education and data center regulation. The IGNITE HBCU Excellence Act would establish competitive grants through the Department of Education for facility improvements at historically Black colleges and universities, prioritizing institutions serving high percentages of Pell Grant-eligible students and those facing declining state support. Separately, Senator Warner's amendment would require large data centers consuming 25 megawatts or more to disclose water usage, electricity consumption, and environmental impacts, with enforcement penalties up to $100,000 daily for violations, while exempting artificial intelligence data centers from certain tax benefits if they achieve high-level green building certification.

The Senate also passed Senate Resolution 913 honoring the life and legacy of former Senator Nancy Kassebaum of Kansas, who served three terms from 1978 to 1997 and was the only woman in the Senate at her election. Kassebaum chaired the Committee on Labor and Human Resources and authored legislation that became the foundation for the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act.
