# Congressional Summary (run on 2026-08-01)

[https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-125/daily-digest](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-125/daily-digest)

On Thursday, July 30, 2026, both chambers of Congress conducted limited business during a brief legislative day, with the Senate introducing thirty-four bills and nine resolutions while passing commemorative measures recognizing National Whistleblower Appreciation Day, American Grown Flower and Foliage Month, National Farmers Market Week, and Gold Star Children's Day. The Senate confirmed numerous military nominations across all service branches and took three record votes on procedural matters, ultimately scheduling a cloture vote for Monday on the AGOA Extension Act, which serves as the legislative vehicle for a continuing resolution. The House met in a pro forma session lasting just two minutes, introducing thirty-six public bills and four resolutions while taking no substantive votes, with multiple members submitting commemorative remarks honoring individuals and organizations.

Senator Schumer introduced the Anti-Corruption Bureau Creation Act alongside Senators Kim, Merkley, and Padilla, proposing the most comprehensive anti-corruption enforcement framework ever advanced by Congress. The bill aims to address alleged unprecedented corruption under the Trump administration, citing estimates that Trump and his family have accumulated over four billion dollars through various business ventures tied to his presidential position. The legislation establishes two primary mechanisms to combat corruption: Title I creates a private right of action allowing citizens and state attorneys general to sue for recovery of corrupted funds with plaintiffs receiving fifteen to thirty percent of recovered amounts, and Title II creates an independent Anti-Corruption Bureau with seven Senate-confirmed commissioners to consolidate functions from the Federal Election Commission, Office of Government Ethics, and Office of Special Counsel.

The Anti-Corruption Bureau features structural protections against presidential interference, including self-sustaining funding, judicial appointment of temporary members to maintain quorum, and explicit criminal penalties for attempts to weaponize the agency. The Bureau would possess exclusive civil enforcement jurisdiction over campaign finance, ethics, conflicts of interest, financial disclosure, and whistleblower protection laws, operating through a self-sustaining Freedom From Influence Fund supported by assessments and settlements. A judicial panel would be created to appoint temporary commissioners if vacancies occur, ensuring the agency maintains operational continuity regardless of presidential obstruction.

Beyond the Anti-Corruption Bureau, the Senate introduced forty-four new bills spanning diverse policy areas including tax administration reforms, school nutrition requirements, defense article transfers for Ukraine, workforce development initiatives, and veterans' services expansion
