# Congressional Summary (run on 2026-11-13)

[https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-170/daily-digest](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-170/daily-digest)

On November 11, 2026, both chambers of Congress conducted significant legislative business with the Senate advancing veterans-related measures and judicial nominations while managing consideration of tax legislation, and the House passing multiple small business bills and veterans' legislation. The Senate passed the Veterans' Compensation Cost-of-Living Adjustment Act and three resolutions honoring veterans and designating National Veterans Small Business Week, while scheduling cloture votes on four federal judicial nominations. The House suspended rules to pass seven bills addressing small business administration matters, including cybersecurity resources, loan modernization, and disaster assistance, along with the Retirement Fairness for Charities and Educational Institutions Act and the Take Care of America's Veterans Act.

House expenditure reports documented numerous congressional delegations visiting countries across Africa, Europe, Asia, and the Americas during the third and fourth quarters of 2026. Various House committees filed reports detailing per diem expenses, transportation costs, and other expenditures totaling hundreds of thousands of dollars, with individual delegations traveling to destinations including Taiwan, France, Hungary, Germany, Sweden, and numerous African nations. The Commission on Security and Cooperation in Europe also reported travel expenses of approximately $102,680 for delegations visiting the Netherlands, Slovenia, Greece, and Bosnia and Herzegovina, with most international transportation costs utilizing military air assets.

The House passed small business support bills focused on modernizing lending programs and protecting enterprises from emerging threats. H.R. 1013, the Retirement Fairness for Charities and Educational Institutions Act, allowed 403(b) retirement plans for nonprofit workers to access collective investment trusts currently available only to 401(k) plans, aiming to reduce fees. While supporters argued this levels the playing field for teachers and nonprofit employees, opponents warned it strips important securities protections, with major labor unions and veterans groups opposing the measure. The bill passed 295-128 with bipartisan support, and the House also advanced legislation addressing cybersecurity for small businesses, disaster loans, and improvements to the SBA's lending programs.

The Senate addressed multiple amendments covering environmental regulation, agency transparency, and veterans' benefits. Amendments focused on Clean Water Act permitting procedures and state water quality certifications, proposing modifications to permit timelines and extending permit validity periods from five to ten years.
