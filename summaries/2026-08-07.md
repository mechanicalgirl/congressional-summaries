# Congressional Summary (run on 2026-08-07)

[https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-128/daily-digest](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-128/daily-digest)

The Senate convened on August 5, 2026, with floor activity dominated by ongoing consideration of the AGOA Extension Act and related appropriations legislation, alongside several nominations and other business items. The Senate confirmed Erica Schwartz as Director of the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention and advanced consideration of en bloc nominations under S. Res. 817, with cloture votes scheduled for the following day. The Senate passed S. 850, the Northern Border Security Enhancement and Review Act, which requires periodic updates to northern border threat analyses and strategy documents, with an amendment accepted to extend reporting deadlines for Department of Homeland Security implementation efforts. A motion to proceed to S.J. Res. 187 concerning EPA PFAS reporting requirements failed on a 48-50 vote after debate between Senators Whitehouse and Capito regarding whether delaying the reporting deadline would impede public access to contamination information or allow necessary system infrastructure completion. The Senate introduced thirty bills and five resolutions covering topics from Social Security reform and Medicare fraud prevention to bus operator safety protections and postal facility designations.

Beyond floor activity, the Senate conducted votes on multiple nominations and legislative matters, with particular focus on contentious confirmations. Senators debated Todd Blanche's nomination for Attorney General, with Democratic critics citing concerns about his handling of January 6th insurrectionist compensation, Epstein files management, tax immunity agreements for the Trump family, and efforts to restrict abortion access nationwide. Democratic senators argued he prioritizes the President's personal interests over the rule of law, while supporters contended he brings integrity and focus to crime-fighting efforts. The Senate also addressed immigration enforcement practices, raising concerns about inadequate vetting of Immigration and Customs Enforcement officers, detainee deaths in custody, and lack of accountability mechanisms. Additional Senate discussion covered economic issues including inflation tied to tariffs and energy policy, hemp-derived THC product loopholes linked to pediatric poisoning cases, government spending transparency, and viral gain-of-function research funding.
