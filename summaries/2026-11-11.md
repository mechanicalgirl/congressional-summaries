# Congressional Summary (run on 2026-11-11)

[https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-168/daily-digest](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-168/daily-digest)

During the 119th Congress's second session, both chambers are actively advancing legislation while managing numerous nominations and committee activities. The Senate convened on November 9 to continue post-cloture consideration of the Survivor Justice Tax Prevention Act on H.R. 2347, which serves as the foundation for the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act, while the House passed several suspensions of rules including the Nuclear REFUEL Act, the American Enrichment Deployment Act, and the Veterans' Compensation Cost-of-Living Adjustment Act of 2026. Through September 30, 2026, the legislative session has produced 50 public laws enacted and 842 total measures passed, with 11,308 executive branch nominations confirmed out of 21,493 received.

The House advanced several nuclear energy regulatory bills aimed at streamlining licensing procedures and modernizing infrastructure oversight. H.R. 3978 excludes spent fuel reprocessing facilities from lengthy production facility licensing by allowing them to be licensed under a more efficient one-step framework, while H.R. 5549 eliminates mandatory hearings on uncontested nuclear license applications. H.R. 9084 requires the Department of Energy to publicly announce nuclear safety standard changes within 72 hours and submit annual reports to Congress, and H.R. 9612 aligns uranium enrichment facility licensing with fuel cycle facility procedures while maintaining full Nuclear Regulatory Commission authority.

Multiple Senate committees filed comprehensive foreign travel expenditure reports for the third quarter of 2026, with notable expenses from the Committee on Armed Services totaling $1,080,530.46 and the Foreign Relations Committee reporting $725,806.97. Additional committee travel expenses included Finance ($603,305.86 total), Appropriations ($289,010.58), Intelligence Committee ($584,105.26), and the U.S. Helsinki Commission ($327,210.87). The Senate also authorized use of the Hart Senate Office Building for Alexander Calder's art installation on November 18, 2026, and passed a concurrent resolution recognizing wage disparities faced by Latina workers, who earn approximately 53-60 cents per dollar earned by white non-Hispanic men.

During the adjournment period, the Senate received numerous executive communications reflecting routine governmental operations, including arms export certifications
