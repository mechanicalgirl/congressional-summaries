# Congressional Summary (run on 2026-09-26)

[https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-152/daily-digest](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-152/daily-digest)

On September 24, 2026, the Senate advanced the Protect College Sports Act with a cloture vote of 74-0 and scheduled final consideration for September 28. The chamber passed several bills including mental health requirements for aviators, FAA medication transparency measures, and federal land trust legislation for the Lower Elwha Klallam Tribe. Additionally, the Senate agreed to resolutions designating Telehealth Awareness Week, a national day of remembrance for nuclear weapons program workers, and Gold Star Families Remembrance Week, though a War Powers Resolution directing removal of armed forces from Iran failed 49-50.

Congress honored distinguished individuals and institutions, including retired Army officer Lieutenant Colonel John David Hawthorne Jr. for his service in the military and community development, Bethesda-Chevy Chase High School for a century of educational excellence, Globe Engineering for 80 years as an aerospace supplier, and various other contributors to military service, cancer research, and business development. The Senate also recognized Indian boarding school survivors on the National Day of Remembrance.

Senator Hoever's John A. Hauser Mental Health in Aviation Act aims to reduce barriers for pilots and air traffic controllers seeking mental health treatment by requiring the FAA to update regulations within 18 months and potentially allowing continued certification while undergoing treatment. The legislation appropriates up to $15 million annually through 2028 for recruiting aviation medical examiners, clearing backlogs, and conducting public awareness campaigns to destigmatize mental health care in the aviation industry.

Senator Reed introduced the Quality Educator Partnership Act, proposing $500 million annually for school-educator preparation partnerships and a National Educator Service Corps to address declining interest in teaching, while Senator Padilla's BASIC Act seeks $1 billion in federal grants to address student food and housing insecurity affecting three in five college students. Ohio and Arizona submitted multiple resolutions to Congress addressing varied concerns including electric aviation integration, NASA relocation, veterans' disability claim processing, federal land mineral access, and AI safety protocols.

The Senate addressed significant national security and infrastructure concerns, including a bipartisan effort to ban Chinese vehicles from U.S. markets, calls to condemn Chinese transnational repression, and warnings about artificial intelligence safety risks before frontier models are released publicly.
