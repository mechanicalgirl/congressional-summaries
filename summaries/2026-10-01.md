# Congressional Summary (run on 2026-10-01)

[https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-154/daily-digest](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-154/daily-digest)

The Senate convened on September 29, 2026, and passed numerous bills and resolutions addressing conservation, infrastructure, and public awareness initiatives, including reauthorization of the Great Lakes Restoration Initiative with $475 million in annual funding through 2030, expansion of tribal access to electronic evidence in criminal investigations, reauthorization of the Traumatic Brain Injury program, and measures supporting rural access to capital and international religious freedom. The Senate also agreed to several resolutions designating awareness months and weeks for various causes, and introduced amendments addressing data center standards, border safety signage, and bilateral coordination with Mexico on serious crimes, with some measures honoring Nicholas Douglas Quets, an American killed by cartel violence in Mexico in 2024.

The Senate advanced several significant initiatives supporting military families and emerging technology oversight. Senator Padilla introduced the BRIDGE Act of 2026, which would establish a three-year pilot program at four military installations to provide comprehensive transition support for servicemembers and their families within one year of separation, including peer-led support groups, spouse employment assistance, and coordination between the Department of Defense and Veterans Affairs. Concurrently, Senator Gallego introduced S. Resolution 918 to establish a Select Committee on Artificial Intelligence within the Senate to conduct comprehensive investigations into AI development, deployment, governance, and risks across national security, cybersecurity, economic impacts, consumer protection, and other critical domains.

The Wildfire Emissions Prevention Act of 2026 represents a bipartisan effort to address wildfire management and air quality challenges in Western states. Championed by Senators Merkley, Padilla, and Curtis, the bill unanimously passed the Environment and Public Works Committee by streamlining prescribed burn regulations under the Clean Air Act, explicitly defining prescribed fires as exceptional events to eliminate state penalties for temporary smoke emissions. The bill also establishes the Smoke-Ready Communities Program to provide grants helping communities prepare for wildfire smoke, recognizing that prevention through prescribed burns causes far less harm than uncontrolled catastrophic fires.

Policy disputes emerged along partisan lines, with Republicans highlighting successes of the Rural Health Transformation Program, which deployed $50 billion across all 50 states, and advancing the Ratepayer Protection Act to prevent large electricity users from shifting grid upgrade costs to consumers.
