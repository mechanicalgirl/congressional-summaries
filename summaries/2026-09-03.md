# Congressional Summary (run on 2026-09-03)

[https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-138/daily-digest](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-138/daily-digest)

On Tuesday, September 1, the House of Representatives convened to introduce 17 new public bills and 3 resolutions while processing committee reports on financial services and consumer protection. The chamber swore in Representative-elect Everton Blair Jr. from Georgia's 13th Congressional District, bringing House membership to 432, and approved several measures through suspended rules votes, including an extension of the African Growth and Opportunity Act with strong bipartisan support and the Northeast Lobsterman Protection Act on a narrower margin. The House also passed ethics-related contempt resolutions regarding Michael Joseph and Hector Roos.

Democratic members held a special order hour to address what they characterized as systemic abuses and deaths within federal immigration detention facilities operated by ICE and CBP. Representatives Torres, Espaillat, and others documented 76 deaths in ICE custody or shortly after release since January 2025, citing inadequate medical care, overcrowded facilities, and unsafe conditions at detention centers. The speakers highlighted specific cases of individuals who died from preventable medical conditions after being denied healthcare and called for accountability, independent investigations, and comprehensive immigration reform.

The House Committee on Ethics advanced contempt proceedings against Michael Joseph for refusing to comply with subpoenas issued during an investigation into allegations involving former Representative Sheila Cherfilus-McCormick. Joseph claimed attorney-client privilege but failed to appear for depositions or produce documents despite multiple warnings, leading the committee to determine his assertion of privilege was invalid and the House to pass a contempt resolution referring the matter to the Department of Justice for prosecution. A similar contempt resolution was also advanced against Hector Roos, a former campaign staffer who refused to comply with subpoenas.

The House adopted a closed rule for floor consideration of four measures, including the Protecting Domestic Mining Act and the Northeast Lobsterman Protection Act. Republican leadership defended the rule and bills as advancing substantive priorities, citing the 119th Congress's record of 899 measures passed and $82 billion in tax relief. Democrats strongly opposed the rule, arguing the bills ignore an affordability crisis while focusing on culture war issues and corporate giveaways, and contending that Trump's tariffs impose greater costs on households than tax cuts provide, ultimately widening wealth inequality.
