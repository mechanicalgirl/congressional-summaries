# Congressional Summary (run on 2026-11-21)

[https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-177/daily-digest](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-177/daily-digest)

On Friday, November 20, the Senate confirmed two federal judges by narrow party-line votes and advanced cloture votes on four additional district court judicial nominees, with final confirmation votes scheduled for no earlier than Monday, November 30. The chamber also passed the "DEATH BETS Act," which prohibits futures contracts based on terrorism, assassination, war, or individual deaths, and approved four ceremonial resolutions recognizing National Native American Heritage Month, Texas Southmost College's centennial, National First-Generation College Celebration Day, and the life of Fiona the hippopotamus of the Cincinnati Zoo.

The Senate concluded a productive legislative session with the passage of the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act of 2026, landmark permitting reform legislation that streamlined environmental review and construction processes after more than a year of intense bipartisan negotiations. The bill passed on a bipartisan vote and represents the culmination of work by an unlikely coalition including Senators Capito, Whitehouse, Lee, and Heinrich, who overcame significant philosophical differences on energy development and environmental protection to reach consensus on core principles allowing faster project construction while maintaining environmental safeguards and regulatory certainty.

Senator Armstrong, an Oklahoma businessman serving a brief 250-day Senate term, delivered a farewell address emphasizing lessons learned about stewardship and working across party lines to accomplish long-term national priorities rather than pursuing short-term political convenience. The Senate also confirmed two federal judicial nominees for the Southern and Northern Districts of Texas and addressed broader Senate business including tributes to departing members, discussion of welfare reform successes from 1996 legislation, recognition of military families, and remarks on various administration oversight matters. Both chambers are scheduled to meet in pro forma sessions on November 24, with no legislative business expected until November 30.
