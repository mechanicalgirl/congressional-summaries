# Congressional Summary (run on 2026-09-02)

[https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-137/daily-digest](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-137/daily-digest)

The Senate met in pro forma session on August 31, 2026, while the House conducted regular business including consideration of multiple technical amendment bills, prison safety enhancements, and medal authorizations under suspension of rules. The House Rules Committee approved rules by a 7-4 vote for four measures scheduled for September 1 floor consideration: the Protecting Domestic Mining Act, Northeast Lobsterman Protection Act, a socialism condemnation resolution, and the Protect Economic and Academic Freedom Act. Additionally, 19 new bills and 4 resolutions were introduced during the session, with extensions of remarks honoring individuals and organizations for community service, military achievements, and significant life milestones. The Senate will remain out of session the week of September 1-4 while the House convenes with an extensive schedule of committee hearings and markups addressing energy policy, education, healthcare, trade, immigration, and intelligence matters.

The House passed several technical correction bills designed to maintain the accuracy and accessibility of the United States Code following previous reorganizations and reclassifications of federal law. H.R. 5185 addressed conforming amendments following Congress's 2011 positive law restatement of Title 41 governing public contracts, updating references across multiple titles by replacing outdated citations with corresponding Title 41 provisions. H.R. 5210 corrected cross-references throughout the Code to reflect reclassifications into titles 2, 50, and 52, affecting provisions related to congressional operations, national security, and elections. H.R. 4465 updated Title 5 to incorporate amendments enacted after October 2021, including revisions to Inspector General authorities, Federal Advisory Committee references, and judicial financial disclosure requirements. H.R. 4523 made technical corrections to Title 49 related to railroad financing programs following the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act. All bills received bipartisan support and passed under suspension of rules.

Additional technical codification efforts included H.R. 4499, which updated statutory references throughout the United States Code to reflect the 2017 reclassification of criminal statutes into Title 34, correcting cross-references across numerous titles without substantively altering existing laws.
