# Congressional Summary (run on 2026-11-22)

[https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-177/daily-digest](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-177/daily-digest)

On November 20, 2026, the Senate confirmed two federal district court judges for Texas positions and advanced cloture votes on five additional judicial nominees as part of efforts to expedite President Trump's judicial appointments before the new Congress convenes. Richard Wesley Bennett was confirmed for the Southern District of Texas by a vote of 49-43, and Courtney Leigh Coker was confirmed for the Northern District of Texas by a vote of 49-41, with confirmation votes on the remaining nominees scheduled for no earlier than November 30. The Senate also passed several resolutions during the session, honoring subjects ranging from Fiona the hippopotamus at the Cincinnati Zoo to National Native American Heritage Month and designating specific days for college celebration awareness and driver safety.

Meanwhile, the House convened briefly on Friday morning to introduce 14 public bills and one resolution while processing various executive communications related to federal regulatory actions. Both chambers scheduled pro forma sessions for November 24 and planned to reconvene the following week to address outstanding legislative priorities including government funding, the National Defense Authorization Act, farm bill provisions, and numerous executive branch nominations.

The Senate also passed the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act of 2026 on November 19, completing over a year of bipartisan negotiations on permitting and energy reform legislation. The bill, developed through collaboration between Senators Capito, Whitehouse, Lee, and Heinrich along with administration officials, seeks to streamline permitting for energy and infrastructure projects while preserving environmental protections. The legislation garnered broad stakeholder support from labor unions, renewable energy developers, oil and gas producers, and consumer advocacy groups, demonstrating consensus across ideological divides. Senator Armstrong, who had served only 250 days since his appointment to fill Oklahoma's seat, delivered a farewell address emphasizing that America's greatest challenges can be resolved through good-faith negotiation and compromise.
