# Congressional Summary (run on 2026-09-19)

[https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-147/daily-digest](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-147/daily-digest)

On September 17, 2026, the Senate conducted limited legislative activity as Congress approached recess, confirming Federal Judge Kasdin Miller Mitchell for the Northern District of Texas and advancing the Protect College Sports Act, which would regulate name, image, and likeness rights for student athletes and govern transfers between institutions. Multiple amendments were filed addressing topics including coach transfer protections, sexual assault investigations, private equity restrictions, and state law preemption. The House held a brief two-minute pro forma session, introducing 33 new bills and 9 resolutions covering veterans benefits, artificial intelligence oversight, tariff policy, and rural broadband services.

A notable amendment by Senator Baldwin proposed prohibiting sports league blackout restrictions, requiring professional sports leagues to provide local fans free access to all games of their local teams through either traditional broadcast or streaming, with enforcement by the FTC, FCC, and state attorneys general. Other amendments primarily addressed technical timing provisions for the Protect College Sports Act, with Senator Thune proposing successive adjustments extending the effective date from three to seven days, while Amendment 6775 carved out exceptions to liability limitations for civil actions involving physical injury, death, sexual abuse, or misconduct.

The comprehensive Amendment 6776 contained the full text of the Protect College Sports Act of 2026, establishing protections for student athletes regarding name, image, and likeness rights, medical coverage, academic protections, and transfer rules, while also addressing college sports broadcasting requirements and funding for historically Black colleges and universities' sports infrastructure. The legislation would establish a Congressional Commission on the Future of College Athletics comprising 24 members tasked with studying athlete compensation alternatives and reviewing health standards over five years, while providing antitrust exemptions for athletic associations enforcing compensation caps and transfer standards, contingent upon establishing implementing bylaws.

Additionally, the Senate unanimously passed H.R. 5345, the Improving Social Security's Service to Victims of Identity Theft Act, and confirmed Mitchell's nomination by a vote of 49-45. The chamber spent considerable time debating amendments to the Protect College Sports Act, filing two cloture motions on the Cruz-Cantwell substitute amendment and underlying bill, while Senator Booker mounted substantial opposition, arguing the legislation granted permanent antitrust exemptions to the NCAA while undermining athletes' legal leverage and favoring institutional interests over player welfare
