# Congressional Summary (run on 2026-07-31)

[https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-124/daily-digest](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-124/daily-digest)

The Senate convened on July 29, 2026, to advance multiple legislative matters including S. Res. 817 on en bloc nominations and the SEED Act, which would extend educator expense deductions to early childhood educators. Through cloture and unanimous consent agreements, the Senate scheduled votes on resolutions regarding child care funding flexibility and military involvement in Iran, while also passing S. Res. 818 designating July as National Anti-Counterfeiting and Consumer Education and Awareness Month. The chamber received testimony from former NIAID Director Anthony Fauci on pandemic response activities and held various committee hearings on artificial intelligence, water rights for Native American tribes, and AI-related senior fraud.

The Senate advanced numerous amendments addressing Russia-related sanctions and trade measures, with Senator Bennet's proposal requesting a comprehensive report on defense systems for co-production with India. The primary Graham-Blumenthal-sponsored measure, the "Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026," would impose broad financial sanctions on Russian entities, restrict energy exports, increase tariffs on Russian goods to 500 percent, and apply duties of up to 100 percent on countries purchasing Russian energy or facilitating sanctions evasion. Proposed counteramendments from Senators Paul, Cantwell, and Welch reflected significant disagreement on the sanctions framework, with additional measures from Senators Cortez Masto, Hirono, and Kennedy addressing related concerns about Russian sanctions compliance and religious persecution in occupied Ukraine territories.

During this period, the Senate advanced procedural matters involving nominations and introduced bills addressing retirement accounts, education data systems, microgrids, whistleblower protections, and nutrient labeling requirements. The State Department submitted numerous arms sales notifications for advanced aircraft and weapons systems to allied nations totaling over $250 million, while the Senate received Federal Aviation Administration directives and regulatory reports from multiple agencies on topics ranging from submarine cable procedures to emissions compliance.

The Senate addressed significant policy disputes spanning foreign affairs, immigration, and veterans' benefits.
