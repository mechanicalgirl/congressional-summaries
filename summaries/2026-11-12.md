# Congressional Summary (run on 2026-11-12)

[https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-170/daily-digest](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-172/issue-170/daily-digest)

On November 11, 2026, the Senate passed veterans-related measures and advanced consideration of the Survivor Justice Tax Prevention Act while scheduling cloture votes on multiple judicial nominations including Richard Wesley Bennett, Courtney Leigh Coker, Kyle Frederick Reardon, and Daniel Lewis Ballou. The House passed seven bills under suspension of rules focused on Small Business Administration matters covering cybersecurity, disaster loans, and loan guaranty programs, along with measures improving veterans' benefits and addressing concurrent receipt of veterans' disability compensation and retired pay.

House expenditure reports for official foreign travel during the third and fourth quarters of 2026 documented delegations to numerous countries worldwide, with the Committee on Foreign Affairs reporting the highest expenditures at $1,157,969.68, followed by the Committee on Ways and Means at $497,359.95 and the Committee on Appropriations at $523,395.50. These reports itemized per diem costs, transportation expenses including military air transportation, and other costs for each delegation member by destination country and travel dates.

The House advanced several small business-focused bills aimed at modernizing the SBA's 504 loan program, supporting manufacturing reshoring, enhancing cybersecurity resources, and improving reporting on small business investment companies. Bipartisan legislation also required the SBA to ensure compliance with veteran business development reporting and establish consistent procedures for disaster loan outreach materials, reflecting congressional focus on strengthening access to capital and supporting veteran entrepreneurs.

Senate amendments to H.R. 2347 addressed permitting procedures and environmental review standards, with Amendments 6891 through 6900 proposing modifications to Clean Water Act certification requirements, NEPA remedies, and regulatory transparency. Key proposals included 90-day application review timelines for state water quality certifications, permit term extensions from five to ten years, and restrictions on injunctive relief pending agency compliance, reflecting disagreements over the scope and pace of environmental permitting.

The Senate advanced the Survivor Justice Tax Prevention Act, which would exclude non-punitive damages from sexual acts or sexual contact from gross income calculations, along with bipartisan permitting reform legislation designed to reduce costly delays in construction and energy sectors.
